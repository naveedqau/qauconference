About The International Conference

Jinnah’s Vision – Pakistan’s Future

The 150th birth anniversary of Quaid-i-Azam Mohammad Ali Jinnah offers an important opportunity to revisit his political career, constitutional struggle, democratic commitments, and leadership in the making of Pakistan. His political journey reflects a significant transformation in modern South Asian history, from an advocate of Indian nationalism to the leading figure of Muslim nationalism.

Beyond the achievement of independence, Jinnah articulated a vision of Pakistan rooted in constitutional governance, equality of citizenship, the rule of law, religious freedom, democratic practice, and respect for fundamental rights. This academic conference seeks to foster scholarly discussion on Jinnah’s role in state formation, his approach to governance, and his aspirations for Pakistan’s future.

Hosted at Quaid-i-Azam University, Islamabad, from December 23–25, 2026, the conference aims to help the nation develop a deeper understanding of how Pakistan can shape its future by drawing upon Jinnah’s constitutional ideals and vision for the state.

Academic Call

Call for Research Papers

Researchers are invited to contribute original scholarly work (abstracts of 250–300 words) addressing the following proposed themes:

  • 01. Historiography on Jinnah: Interpretations, Research Gaps, and Emerging Perspectives.
  • 02. Jinnah’s Personal Life: Experiences, Influences, and Political Evolution.
  • 03. Jinnah’s Leadership: Charisma, Strategy, Negotiations, and Electoral Politics.
  • 04. Jinnah and the All-India Muslim League: Party Organization, Institutional Development, and Patronage of the Muslim Political Demands.
  • 05. Jinnah and Pakistan Movement: Constitutional Struggle, Democratic Principles, Political Mobilization, and the Role of Muslim-Majority Provinces.
  • 06. Jinnah’s and Making of the New State: Partition, Territorial Integration, and Challenges of Independence.
  • 07. Jinnah’s Vision of Statehood: State Formation, Institutional Development, and Nation-Building.
  • 08. Jinnah’s Vision of Governance: Constitutionalism, Democratic Values, the Rule of Law, Fundamental Rights, and Representative Institutions.
  • 09. Jinnah’s Vision of Federalism: Citizenship, Provincial Autonomy, National Integration, and Centre-Province Relations.
  • 10. Jinnah’s Vision of Inclusivity: Religious Freedom, Minority Rights, Gender Equality, and Equal Citizenship.
  • 11. Jinnah’s Socio-economic Vision: Economic Self-Reliance, Social Justice, Education, Youth, and National Development.
  • 12. Jinnah’s Foreign Policy Vision: Sovereignty, Diplomacy, National Security, International Cooperation, and Cordial Relations.
  • 13. Jinnah’s Legacy and Contemporary Relevance: Reinterpreting His Vision, National Challenges, and Pakistan’s Future.
